# Taco Tuesday Consulting — Brand

## The tension the brand has to solve

The name is playful and unmistakably Mexican. The buyer is a CTO or a VP of Engineering
in the US or Canada deciding whether to trust you with hiring their team.

If the identity leans into the joke — a cartoon taco, sombrero, flag colours — the product
reads as a side project and the name becomes a liability. If it erases the origin entirely,
you throw away the one thing that differentiates you from every other staffing platform:
**you are actually here, in Jalisco, and that is the point.**

So the rule is: **professional register, regional warmth.** The seriousness comes from the
typography, the spacing and the restraint. The origin comes from the colour and the mark.

---

## The mark

An **agave** rosette. Five blades converging on a single base.

Why an agave and not a taco:

- **It is Jalisco, precisely.** Zapopan sits in the agave landscape. It is specific to where
  the company actually is, not a generic "Mexico" symbol.
- **It reads at 16 px.** A taco does not — it turns into a brown smudge in a browser tab.
- **The structure says the right thing.** Many blades, one base: distributed talent,
  one company standing behind it. That reading is available if someone asks, and invisible
  if nobody does. Symbols that need explaining are bad symbols.
- **It ages.** An agave takes years to mature. A staffing brand that says "slow, deliberate,
  worth waiting for" is saying something a hiring manager wants to hear.

The centre blade is **maíz** gold; the rest are cream. That gives the mark a warm core at any
size, and it is the only place the two brand colours meet directly.

## The wordmark

`TACO` in ink, `TUESDAY` in agave, `CONSULTING` small and letterspaced underneath.

Splitting the two words in colour does the same job the brand does: it keeps the playful half
and the professional half visible at the same time, without either one winning.

> ⚠️ The logo files use live `<text>` with a font stack. **Before any external use — a
> proposal, a deck, a client's site — convert the text to outlines.** Otherwise the wordmark
> renders in a fallback font on any machine without Outfit installed, and your logo is no
> longer your logo. In Inkscape: Path → Object to Path. In Figma: Outline stroke / flatten.

## Rules

**Clear space.** Leave at least the height of the isotype's rounded corner (¼ of the mark)
free on every side. Nothing sits inside that box — not text, not a border, not another logo.

**Minimum size.** Horizontal lockup: 120 px wide. Below that the word CONSULTING becomes
noise; use the isotype alone instead.

**Do not:**

- Recolour the mark outside the palette. There is no purple version.
- Put the colour isotype on an agave-coloured background. Use `isotype-mono` in cream instead.
- Add a drop shadow, gradient, bevel or outline to the mark.
- Stretch, rotate or squash it. The blades are symmetric on purpose.
- Rewrite the wordmark in another typeface "just for this one slide".

## Voice

The product's actual differentiator, in Gio's own words:

> *"¿Odias que los reclutas te ghosteen? Deja que ellos te busquen."*

That sentence is the brand. Direct, a little irreverent, and it names a real frustration
instead of selling a feature. In English, keep the register: **plain, specific, slightly
blunt** — not corporate ("synergistic talent solutions") and not startup-cute ("we ✨ match ✨
you").

Concrete rules:

- Say what happens, not what it enables. *"Companies have 48 hours to send feedback"*,
  not *"we foster transparent communication"*.
- Numbers over adjectives. *"Response rate: 92%"* beats *"highly responsive"*.
- Never blame the candidate. The whole premise is that the current process fails them.

# Taco Tuesday Consulting — Voice and tone

> **Status:** decided 29-sep-2026 by Gio ("sigamos con el 'tú'"). This file is the written
> version of that decision, so the next person writing copy does not have to re-derive it.
> The product surface is written in **Spanish (es-MX)**, so the examples below are in Spanish.

---

## The one rule everything else hangs from

**Always "tú". Never "usted", never "ustedes".** Everywhere: landing, forms, error messages,
confirmation emails, buttons, empty states.

Four reasons, in the order they matter:

1. **"Tú" is the product norm in Mexican tech.** Kavak, Clip, Bitso, Nubank and Mercado Libre
   all use it, and all of them sell to companies too. In a Mexican tech landing in 2026,
   "usted" does not read as formal — it reads as *old*.
2. **The name already picked the register.** A product called *Taco Tuesday* that then
   addresses you as "usted" sounds like a costume. Nobody can name the inconsistency, but
   everybody feels it.
3. **The promise is anti-corporate.** The copy says "sin silencios", "aunque sea un no",
   "sin letras chiquitas". The whole argument is *you will not be treated like a case file*.
   "Usted" is precisely the register of the HR department that left you on read. The pronoun
   would contradict the pitch.
4. **One page, one voice.** A visitor sees the company section AND the candidate section —
   they sit one below the other. Tutear the candidate and ustedear the company and anyone
   who scrolls sees two voices and a site written by two people who do not talk.

---

## The five rules that go with it

| # | Rule | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Always "tú".** No "usted" anywhere in the product. | Above. |
| 2 | **Company sections: "tú", concrete, with numbers.** No slang, no jokes at the client's expense. | This is where the money is. |
| 3 | **Candidate sections: "tú", warmer.** Here you may name the pain directly (ghosting) — it is the reader's own pain. | Naming it is the differentiator. |
| 4 | **"Nosotros" only to commit, never to boast.** "te escribimos", "no te hacemos perder el tiempo" — never "somos líderes en…". | A promise is checkable; a boast is not. |
| 5 | **The impersonal is banned as an escape hatch.** Never "se le notificará", "será contactado". | When you do not know how to say something, the impersonal sounds corporate and evasive — exactly what the product promises not to be. |

### The one exception: the privacy notice

Legal convention there is impersonal. The notice as written **tutea** ("tu correo", "puedes
pedirnos") and it stays that way on purpose: a notice that is understood is worth more than one
that sounds like a lawyer. This is a decision, not an oversight — revisit it if a lawyer
reviews the notice and disagrees.

---

## The real risk, and how to cover it

The risk is **not** the pronoun. If you ever sell to a large, traditional enterprise, what will
cost you is sounding *lightweight*.

The fix is not switching to "usted". It is **raising the register without raising the
formality**: same "tú", but concrete nouns and numbers instead of jokes.

```
"Tú" + precise  = modern professional.          ✅
"Tú" + jokey    = a startup you would not trust  ❌
                  with a hiring budget.
```

### Examples

| ❌ Do not write | ✅ Write |
| --- | --- |
| "Será contactado por nuestro equipo." | "Te escribimos en menos de 48 horas." |
| "Somos líderes en reclutamiento tech." | "Llevamos 40 procesos cerrados este año." |
| "¿Le interesa conocer más?" | "¿Quieres ver cómo funciona?" |
| "Su solicitud ha sido recibida." | "Ya estás en la lista." |
| "¡Ándale, no te quedes fuera! 🌮" | "Te avisamos en cuanto abramos." |

The last row is the one people get wrong: it obeys rule 1 and still breaks rule 2.

---

## How to check a new piece of copy

Four questions, in order. Any "no" means rewrite.

1. Does it address the reader as "tú"?
2. Would it survive being read out loud by a CTO to their boss? *(register)*
3. Does every "nosotros" carry a promise you can be held to? *(rule 4)*
4. Is there an impersonal construction hiding a sentence you did not want to write? *(rule 5)*
